# The care team: therapist, dietitian, pediatrician, and the family

Status: **proposal; part of the FBT framework** (`2026-11-27-fbt-framework.md`), which supersedes this doc's
data model and build order. The roles and the five rules below still stand. Written 2026-11-27. Nothing here is built. It changes the data model, so it needs a
human's approval (AGENTS.md: "Do not refactor storage without an approved plan"), and the role details need a
clinician to check them. Where this doc says what a role needs, that is my reasoning about how family-based
treatment (FBT) teams usually work, not something a clinician has confirmed yet.

## Why this matters

Today a family links to clinicians one at a time, and every clinician gets the same screens and the same
write access. Real treatment for a teen with anorexia is usually run by a small team:

- a **therapist** who leads FBT and sees the family every week;
- a **dietitian** who advises on what meals should look like;
- a **pediatrician** or adolescent-medicine doctor who checks the teen is medically safe;
- sometimes a **psychiatrist** (medication, other conditions) and a **care coordinator** at the clinic.

Two things go wrong when a tool ignores that:

1. **Every role wades through everything.** A pediatrician seeing the family once a month does not need
   Tuesday's snack. They need "anything medically worrying since my last visit".
2. **The family hears more than one voice.** If the therapist and the dietitian can both edit the same
   instructions, parents get contradicting advice. FBT depends on the team speaking with one voice, so the
   app should make it obvious who owns each instruction.

## The roles

| Role | Sees the family | First question when they open the app | Owns (can change) | Reads |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Therapist (FBT lead) | Weekly | "How did meals go since the last session, and what should we work on?" | Care plan (table lines, refused-meal steps), challenge foods, treatment phase, session notes | Everything |
| Dietitian | Every 1 to 4 weeks | "Is the family serving enough, regular, varied meals?" | Nutrition targets, supplements, whether parents see numbers, food types (with the therapist) | Meals, foods, nutrition, timing, supplements; notes |
| Pediatrician | Every 1 to 4 weeks, more if unstable | "Anything medically worrying since I last saw them?" | Warning signs and who to call; medical notes | Red flags, refusals, supplements, a summary since last visit |
| Psychiatrist | Monthly or less | "How is distress trending?" | Their own notes | Distress, summary, notes |
| Care coordinator | When enrolling | "Who is in this family's team, and are they set up?" | Enrolment, invites, team membership | Names and team only, **no meal data** |

The family (one or more caregivers) owns its own logs and notes, and can remove any team member at any time.
That stays true for every role.

## How it should feel to use: five rules

1. **One shared record, a different first screen per role.** Same data underneath; each role opens on the
   view that answers its first question. Nobody loses access to the rest; it's one tap away.
2. **"Since you last saw them", not "this week".** Each team member's summary covers the time since their
   own last session with the family. A weekly therapist and a monthly pediatrician both get a complete
   picture with no gaps and no repeats. The clinician marks a session done in one tap, which starts the
   next window.
3. **Every instruction shows who owns it.** The care plan, targets and warning signs each carry their
   owner's role ("From your dietitian"). Only the owner (or the therapist as lead) can change them, so the
   family never sees two versions.
4. **Alerts go to the right person.** A request for support goes to the therapist first. A medical warning
   sign (fainting, chest pain, refusing food or drink for a day) goes to the pediatrician as well, and always
   shows the family the fixed "call now" message. Nobody is alerted about something outside their role.
5. **No double documentation.** Clinicians already write notes in their clinic's record system. The app gives
   them a one-page summary to paste or print (the session summary, built), not a second place to write everything.

## What each role's first screen shows

- **Therapist:** today's Overview: "Worth talking about", the week grid, the care plan the family sees.
- **Dietitian:** meal regularity and timing, food variety and types, nutrition ranges (phase 2 data),
  supplements given, and the targets they set.
- **Pediatrician:** a short medical panel: warning signs reported, days with refused meals, supplements
  missed, a one-line summary since their last visit, and the warning-signs card they control. If clinicians
  want it, a clinician-only place to record weight and vitals from clinic visits (see decisions; never shown
  to the family).
- **Psychiatrist:** distress trend and the summary.
- **Coordinator:** the team list and invite status, nothing clinical.

| Improvement | Why | Where it goes |

| **Billing (remote therapeutic monitoring codes)** | If a clinic can bill for it, the pitch changes. Unverified that these codes apply. | Roadmap A5, a question for a clinic's billing person. No code. |
